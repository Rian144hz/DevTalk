# Privacy policy

Version 2026-10-26. This is a plain-language draft for Brazil's data protection law (LGPD). It has not been reviewed by a lawyer.

## The short version

- Your practice (your answers, the coach's feedback, your expressions and reviews) stays **on your computer**. It is sent only to the coach (AI provider) you chose in Settings, when you ask for feedback.
- The community keeps only what it needs: your e-mail, your profile, your private messages, which voice rooms you were in and when, and your safety actions (blocks and reports).
- **Private messages** are stored on the community server so the two of you can read them. Only you and the other person read a conversation. Moderators see a message only when someone in the conversation reports it, together with the 10 messages before it.
- **Voice rooms**: your voice goes directly between the computers in the room. It is **never recorded, never transcribed and never passes through the community server**. Because the connection is direct, the other people in the room can see your IP address. Moderators see a room only when someone reports it: its title, theme, host, who was there and when. They never hear it.
- **Signing in**: you can use a code sent to your e-mail or your **GitHub account**. With GitHub you sign in on github.com, in your own browser, and DevTalk never sees your GitHub password. GitHub shares your name, username, profile picture and e-mail with the community server. Your name and picture are only suggestions for your profile: they are saved there only if you keep them.
- For your practice graph and badges, the app sends **numbers only**: how many answers and reviews you did each day, and a few milestones. Never what you wrote.
- You can see, export and delete your community data in the app at any time.

## What we collect for the community

- **E-mail address**: the one you give to get sign-in codes, or the one GitHub shares when you sign in with GitHub. Other members never see it.
- **GitHub sign-in details**, only if you sign in with GitHub: GitHub shares your name, username, profile picture and e-mail with the community server (Supabase), which keeps them with your sign-in record. DevTalk asks GitHub only to read your profile and e-mail (the permissions read:user and user:email). It cannot see your repositories or change anything on GitHub. Your name and picture are only a suggestion for your profile: they are copied there, and your picture is stored, only if you keep them.
- **Profile**: display name, native language, English level, time zone, area, stack (and up to 2 main technologies), available times, bio, an optional photo, an optional banner and the default picture you chose.
- **Practice counts**: for each day of the last 400 days, how many answers and how many reviews you did in the app. Only the two numbers, never the text.
- **Milestones**: when your level goes up in the app (for example B1 to B2) and when you have saved 50 or 100 expressions.
- **Whether you use Linux**, to show the "Linux native" badge.
- **Badges given by moderators** (Beta tester, Mentor, Open source contributor), with the reason, in the moderation records.
- **Acceptances**: which version of the terms, this policy and the code of conduct you accepted, your confirmation that you are 18 or older, and when.
- **Follows and likes**: whom you follow, who follows you and the profiles you like, with the date of each.
- **Private messages**: the conversations you start or accept, the messages you send, the corrections you suggest and the Thanks you give, with their dates, and which messages you have read. A message request you decline is kept but hidden from you.
- **Corrections count**: how many corrections you gave each day, for your practice graph.
- **Voice rooms**: the rooms you open (title, theme, suggested level, who created it, who hosts it, when it opened and ended) and each time you are in a room: when you joined and left, your room role, whether you were removed, whether your microphone is muted and whether the host or a room moderator asked you to mute. We keep this for reports, for badges and for your data export.
- **Connection messages** that the computers in a room send each other to start a call. They contain network addresses (IP addresses). Only the person a message is addressed to can read it.
- **Limit counts**: to limit abuse, the community server also keeps the times of your room actions for about a minute, and how many connection messages you sent in each hour for about a day, without what they contained.
- **Blocks** you make and **reports** you send.
- **Moderation notices** sent to you, and moderation actions about your account.

We do not collect your practice text, your voice, your contacts or your location. Your voice in a room is never recorded, never transcribed and never sent to the community server. The app asks your system only for the microphone; the camera and the screen are never used. Your computer measures how loud each voice in the room is, only to show who is speaking; nothing from it is saved or sent. "Check with the coach" in a chat sends only the draft you are writing to the AI provider you chose; nothing from it is saved, and the other person's messages are never sent. Before your photo or a still banner leaves your computer, the app re-encodes it, which removes hidden data such as GPS position. A picture suggested by GitHub is re-encoded the same way. For an animated banner (GIF or WebP), the app removes comments and EXIF/XMP data before sending it, without changing the frames.

## What other members see

- Your profile, photo or default picture, banner, and these badges: Creator, Moderator, Founding member, Beta tester, Mentor, Open source contributor, Diff master (25 of your corrections received Thanks), Room host (5 rooms you opened where someone else joined), Active speaker (10 times in a room for 5 minutes or more with someone else), your area, Linux native and your main technologies.
- Your **practice graph and progress badges** (day streaks, level up, Vocabulary hunter), unless you turn off "Show my practice graph and progress badges to other members" in People › Edit profile. When it is off, only you see them; the daily numbers are still kept with your account so your own graph works.
- **Whom you follow and who follows you** (the lists and their counts), and **how many likes your profile has**. The person you follow sees the date in their notice and in their data export; other members do not see when you followed or liked someone.
- You appear in the **member list**, where members search by name, English level, area and technology. Turn off "Show me in the member list" in People › Edit profile to leave it; members can still open your profile from a follower list or a link.
- Members never see your e-mail, your practice text, your coach's corrections or your reviews.
- If you signed in with GitHub, members see only what you kept in your profile, such as the name and picture GitHub suggested. DevTalk does not show your GitHub username or a link to your GitHub account.
- **In a private conversation**, the other person sees your messages, the corrections you suggest and your Thanks. Nobody else does. If one of you blocks the other, the conversation closes for both; it is kept, and it opens again if the block is removed.
- **In a voice room**, the other people there hear you and see your name, picture, level, room role and whether your microphone is muted. They can also see your **IP address**, because their computers connect directly to yours: it can show roughly where you are and who your internet provider is. Any member can see the room list: a room's title, theme, suggested level, host and the names and pictures of the people in it. A room is hidden from a member who has a block with someone in it, either way.
- The Room host and Active speaker badges are worked out from who was in which room and when, never from what was said.

## Why, and on what legal basis

- To run the community you asked to join: your account and sign-in (by e-mail code or GitHub), your profile, private chat, voice rooms and the safety tools (LGPD art. 7, V: performing the service you requested).
- Your consent, given when you join (LGPD art. 7, I). You can withdraw it by deleting your account.
- To keep members safe: reports, the copy of reported messages, the room details a report shows and moderation records (LGPD art. 7, IX: legitimate interest in a safe community).

## Who processes the data

- **Supabase** stores the community data on servers in **São Paulo, Brazil**. It acts as our processor (operador). It also receives what GitHub shares when you sign in with GitHub.
- **Google (Gmail)** delivers the sign-in code e-mails, when you sign in with an e-mail code.
- **GitHub** runs the sign-in page when you choose GitHub. You type your GitHub password only on github.com, never in DevTalk. GitHub's own terms and privacy statement apply to it, and it knows that DevTalk asked to read your profile and e-mail. When DevTalk prepares your profile suggestion, GitHub's image server (avatars.githubusercontent.com) sends your profile picture to your computer.
- **Cloudflare** runs a public STUN server (stun.cloudflare.com). When your computer connects to the others in a room, the app asks it which public address your computer has, to find a connection path. It sees your IP address. It does not get your voice or anything else about the room.
- The **AI provider you choose** (for example Google Gemini) receives the practice text you submit for feedback, under that provider's own terms. This happens only when you ask the coach for feedback, or when you choose "Check with the coach" for a chat draft (only that draft).

We do not sell your data and we do not use it for advertising.

## How long we keep it

- Your community data is kept while your account exists.
- Messages stay until you delete them. Deleting one of your messages removes its text and its corrections; the other person sees "Message deleted". If the message was already part of a report, the copy kept for the moderators stays.
- The rooms you opened and your sessions in rooms are kept while your account exists. A room and its sessions are also deleted if the person who opened it deletes their account. Connection messages are deleted when the room ends, and normally about 5 minutes after they were sent (the clean-up runs whenever someone in a room checks in).
- When you delete your account, your profile, photo, banner, practice counts, milestones, badges, follows, followers, likes, acceptances, blocks, notices, **all your conversations** (the messages and corrections in them, from both sides), the rooms you opened, your sessions in rooms, your connection messages and the GitHub details kept with your sign-in record are deleted.
- Reports that other people sent about you are kept for moderation, without your account attached, and so is the copy of a reported conversation (it keeps the text, but not who wrote it once that account is deleted). Reports you sent are kept without your name. The room details in a report are read from the room itself, and no copy is made; they are gone when the room is deleted.

## Your rights under the LGPD

You can, at any time:

- **See and correct** your profile: People › Edit profile.
- **Get a copy** of your data (portability): Settings › Account › Export my data. It includes your conversations, the messages you sent and the corrections you gave (not the other person's messages), the rooms you opened and your own sessions in rooms (not other people's).
- **Delete** your account and your data: Settings › Account › Delete my account.
- **Withdraw consent**: delete your account.
- **Remove DevTalk's access to your GitHub account**: in GitHub, Settings › Applications › Authorized OAuth Apps. Deleting your DevTalk account does not do this for you.
- **Ask questions** about how we use your data, or ask for anything above by e-mail.

## Security

Your sign-in session is stored encrypted with your system keyring. The community database lets each person read and change only what the rules allow.

When you sign in with GitHub, your browser sends a one-time code back to DevTalk through a local address on your own computer (127.0.0.1). That address is open only while you sign in (at most 5 minutes), only your own computer can reach it, and the code is useless without a secret that stays inside DevTalk.

## Age

The community is only for people who are 18 or older. If we learn that an account belongs to someone younger, we delete it.
