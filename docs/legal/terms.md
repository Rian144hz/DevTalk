# Terms of use

Version 2026-10-26. This is a plain-language draft. It has not been reviewed by a lawyer.

## What DevTalk is

DevTalk is a free desktop app that helps developers practise English. The practice coach works on your computer without an account. The community part (profiles, the member list, private chat and voice rooms) needs an account.

## Who can use the community

- You must be **18 or older**.
- You need a way to sign in: a working e-mail address (we send a code to it each time you sign in) or a GitHub account with an e-mail address.
- One account per person. Do not pretend to be someone else.

## Your account

- You can sign in in two ways: with a **code we send to your e-mail**, or with your **GitHub account**. DevTalk has no password.
- With GitHub you sign in on github.com, in your own browser. DevTalk never sees your GitHub password. GitHub shares your name, username, profile picture and e-mail with the community server. Your name and picture are only suggestions for your profile, and they are saved only if you keep them (see the [Privacy policy](#/legal/privacy)).
- If your GitHub e-mail is the same as the e-mail of a DevTalk account, both ways sign in to the same account.
- Keep your e-mail account and your GitHub account safe: anyone who can read your e-mail, or sign in to your GitHub account, can sign in as you.
- You can remove DevTalk's access to your GitHub account in GitHub: Settings › Applications › Authorized OAuth Apps.
- You can sign out, export your data and delete your account at any time in **Settings › Account**.

## What you share

- Your profile (name, photo, banner, level, languages, time zone, stack, available times, bio and badges) is visible to other signed-in members. Your practice graph and progress badges are too, unless you hide them in People › Edit profile.
- Whom you follow, who follows you and how many likes your profile has are visible to other signed-in members. Following someone tells them once a week at most.
- **Private messages** are read only by you and the other person. A conversation starts with a request: your first message, which the other person can accept or decline. You can send at most 10 requests, 500 messages and 200 corrections a day.
- You can suggest a correction to the other person's messages and they can thank you for it. You can delete your own messages; their text and corrections go with them.
- If someone in a conversation reports a message, the moderators see that message and the 10 before it.
- **Voice rooms.** Any member can open a room now, with a title, a theme and a suggested level. Up to 6 people can be in a room, and each person can be in one room at a time. A room stays open until it is empty or the host ends it. You can open at most 10 rooms a day.
- Your voice goes directly between the computers in the room. DevTalk does not record it, transcribe it or send it through the community server. The other people in a room can see your IP address, because their computers connect directly to yours.
- Any member can see a room's title, theme, suggested level, host and the names and pictures of the people in it. The community server keeps who was in each room and when, for reports, badges and your data export.
- Your photo and banner must follow the code of conduct like everything else you share; an offensive banner can be reported with the profile.
- Share only what you are comfortable with. Do not put phone numbers, addresses or other private details in your profile.
- You must follow the [Code of conduct](#/legal/conduct).

## Moderation

Members can block and report each other, and report a single message or a person in a voice room. Blocking closes your conversation for both of you. Moderators read reports and can warn or suspend an account, for a few days or for good. A suspended account cannot edit its profile or send reports, and other members cannot see it.

In a voice room:

- The **host** is the person who opened the room, or who was given the role. The host can give and take away the room moderator role, pass the host role to someone in the room and end the room. If the host leaves, the role goes to a room moderator, or else to the person who has been there longest.
- The host and the room moderators can **mute** someone, who can unmute themselves, and **remove** someone, who cannot come back to that room. Nobody can mute or remove the host, and a room moderator cannot remove another room moderator.
- Everyone can mute someone for themselves only, block and report. If you block someone who is in your room, you leave the room.
- A report from a room shows the moderators the room's title, theme, host, who was there and when. It never includes audio, because voice is not recorded.
- Moderators of the app have no special power inside a room. They act on reports.

## No guarantees

DevTalk is a small free project. The community server can be slow, asleep or unavailable, and features can change. Voice rooms connect computers directly and use no relay server, so a call can fail on some networks. Signing in with GitHub depends on GitHub and on your web browser, so it can fail; the e-mail code is the other way in. We do our best to keep your data safe, but we cannot promise the service will always work.

## Changes

If these terms change, the app shows the new version and asks you to accept it again.
